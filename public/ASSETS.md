## Hero video — house construction

Referenced by `lib/constants.ts` -> `HERO_VIDEO`, and shown in the hero's media
panel, which expands to full-bleed as the visitor scrolls.

### What the footage has to show

The hero is the claim "We draw it. We build it. You move in." The footage has to
be the middle of that sentence, so shoot or licence **house construction, not a
finished building**:

- A residential structure mid-build. Blockwork, formwork, a rising concrete
  frame, roof timbers going on, scaffolding.
- People working. An empty site reads as a stalled project.
- Slow camera movement only. A push-in, a slow pan, or a locked-off shot with
  movement inside the frame. Handheld and fast cuts fight the type above it.

Avoid drone establishing shots of finished estates, stock footage of people in
hard hats pointing at clipboards, and anything with a visible other-brand logo.

### Where to get it

Own footage from a live site is best, and for a construction firm it is the
cheapest differentiator on the page. Failing that, these are free and licensed
for commercial use: **Pexels Videos**, **Coverr**, and **Mixkit** — search
"house construction", "bricklaying", "concrete pour", "roof framing".

### What ships today

`videos/hero.mp4` is exactly this: workers on a house under construction, 9
seconds, 1280x720, 2.7 MB. Replace it with footage of your own site the moment
you have any — on a construction firm's website, your own work outperforms
anything licensed.

### Replacing it

| File | Target |
| --- | --- |
| `videos/hero.mp4` | H.264, 1280x720 or 1920x1080, **under 4 MB**, no audio, 8-15s loop. |
| `videos/hero.webm` | Optional VP9 encode. Set `HERO_VIDEO.webm` in `lib/constants.ts` once it exists — it is not rendered while null, so no failed request. |
| `images/hero-poster.jpg` | The video's first frame. This is the LCP image; keep it under 200 KB. |

720p is deliberate. The video sits behind a scrim at partial opacity, so the
resolution is not doing perceptual work — the file size is. 1080p of the same
clip was 30 MB against 2.7 MB for 720p, for no visible difference in place.

Encoding:

```bash
# MP4 — faststart moves the index to the front so playback starts immediately
ffmpeg -i source.mov -an -c:v libx264 -crf 26 -preset slow \
  -vf "scale=1920:-2" -movflags +faststart public/videos/hero.mp4

# WebM
ffmpeg -i source.mov -an -c:v libvpx-vp9 -crf 34 -b:v 0 \
  -vf "scale=1920:-2" public/videos/hero.webm

# Poster from the first frame
ffmpeg -i public/videos/hero.mp4 -vframes 1 -q:v 3 public/images/hero-poster.jpg
```

### If you ship without it

Nothing breaks. The frame falls back to the poster, and beneath that to a drawn
setting-out grid (`.bg-blueprint` in `styles/globals.css`) so an assetless build
looks like an architectural drawing rather than a failed `<video>` element.

### Contrast

The headline sits on the footage, so the hero applies a bottom-weighted scrim
that reaches 75% opacity at the base. That is frame-independent — it holds the
copy above 4.5:1 over a blown-out sky as well as a dark interior — but a clip
that is uniformly bright across the lower third will still look washed. Prefer
footage with some depth in the bottom half.
